About

Semantic Art Search lets you explore open museum collections by describing what you're looking for. You don't need to know a title, an artist or the words a museum used to catalogue a work.

I'm Kristian Møller Schmidt, and I built this to bring the power of semantic search to art, across museums and in a way that's fun to use. It's free and open source, and it isn't affiliated with any of the museums.

How the search works

Most traditional art search engines look for your words in titles and catalogue records. Here, every image and every search is converted to a vector, a list of numbers that captures what it shows or means. The results are the images whose vectors lie closest to your search. If a cat appears in a painting, a search for "cat" has a good chance of finding it, even if the museum's record never mentions one. You can also try broader ideas, such as battle at sea or mourning. You can search in many languages, or upload an image of your own to find artworks that resemble it.

Results are ranked by how closely they match, not filtered by an exact match, so some will be surprising and some simply wrong. Title Search matches on titles instead, which helps when you know roughly what a work is called.

The art map

This map uses the same vectors as the search to lay out the whole collection, with works that look alike placed near each other. Colour it by museum, work type or century for an overview of how the artworks cluster and where each group falls, or simply wander through it without searching for anything.

You can also search from the map to see where the matches gather, or use "Show on map" on any artwork to find its place in the collection. Open the art map.

The site includes works the museums have released as public domain or open access, and focuses on visual art such as paintings, prints and drawings, so it covers only part of each collection. Titles, dates and other details come from the museums' data. Each artwork links back to its page at the museum, which is the place to check them.

Generated descriptions

The descriptions you can request on an artwork are generated automatically by a language model. They can contain mistakes, so don't treat them as scholarship.

Your searches and images

There are no user accounts. The site records the text and filters of each new search, with the time, so I can see how it's used. That record doesn't include your IP address. Your IP address is used briefly to limit how many requests can be made, and may appear in the web server's ordinary logs.

To run a search, your text or image is sent to an external service, which converts it to a vector. This site doesn't store images you upload, but they do pass through that service, so please avoid uploading anything private.

For developers

The source code is on GitHub. There's also a rate-limited JSON API: /api/search/?query=... searches the collection, and /api/museums/ lists the museums.
